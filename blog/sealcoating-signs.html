5 Signs Your Asphalt Driveway Needs Sealcoating

Protect your investment before small damage becomes a costly replacement.

Gettis Bros Team • 5 min read

Your asphalt driveway takes a beating every single day. Between vehicle traffic, UV exposure, and the constant rain and salt air that come with living on the Oregon Coast, the surface slowly breaks down whether you notice it or not. The good news is that sealcoating is one of the most cost-effective ways to extend the life of your driveway and avoid a full replacement down the road.

But how do you know when it is time? Here are five warning signs that your driveway is overdue for a fresh sealcoat — and why acting early can save you thousands.

1. The Color Has Faded from Black to Gray

When your driveway was first paved, it had that rich, deep black color. Over time, ultraviolet rays from the sun oxidize the asphalt binder, causing the surface to fade to a dull gray. This is not just cosmetic. That color change means the protective oils in the asphalt are drying out, leaving the surface brittle and vulnerable to cracking.

Here on the Oregon Coast, the UV damage might surprise you. Even with our overcast days, UV radiation still reaches the surface year-round. Properties in Newport, Lincoln City, and Depoe Bay that face south or west tend to fade faster because of increased sun exposure during breaks in the clouds. If your driveway has turned noticeably gray, sealcoating will restore the protective layer and bring back that fresh, dark finish.

2. Cracks Are Starting to Appear

Small hairline cracks might not look like much, but they are the beginning of serious damage. Water seeps into those cracks, sits beneath the surface, and weakens the base material underneath. When temperatures drop — and coastal Oregon gets plenty of near-freezing nights in winter — that trapped water expands and makes the cracks worse.

There are two patterns to watch for. Spider cracking looks like a web of thin lines spreading across the surface. Alligator cracking is a more advanced pattern where the cracks interconnect in a way that resembles reptile skin. Spider cracking is usually a sign that sealcoating is overdue. Alligator cracking means the damage has reached the base layer, and you may need patching or repair work before the sealcoat can do its job.

Either way, the earlier you address cracks, the less expensive the fix. A sealcoat fills minor surface cracks and prevents water from penetrating further.

3. Water Is Pooling on the Surface

After a rainstorm, walk out and look at your driveway. If you see standing water that takes more than a few hours to drain, that is a red flag. Pooling water means the surface has eroded enough to create low spots, or the original drainage grading has shifted over time.

In Lincoln County, this is a particularly common problem. We average over 70 inches of rain per year, and driveways that are not properly maintained get pounded relentlessly from October through April. Standing water accelerates every other form of damage — it feeds cracks, washes away loose aggregate, and promotes moss growth that further deteriorates the surface.

Sealcoating creates a smooth, water-resistant barrier that helps rainwater sheet off the surface instead of soaking in. For driveways with drainage issues, we often recommend addressing the grading first and then applying a sealcoat to protect the repaired surface. If you are noticing persistent puddles, reach out for a free assessment so we can evaluate whether the problem is surface-level or structural.

4. The Surface Feels Rough and Rocky

Run your hand across a section of your driveway, or just look closely. If you can see and feel individual stones and aggregate poking through the surface, the asphalt binder has worn away. This condition is called aggregate exposure, and it means the glue that holds everything together is breaking down.

You might also notice small loose stones collecting at the edges of your driveway or in your garage. That is material literally crumbling off the surface. Every bit of aggregate that comes loose leaves the remaining surface weaker and more porous.

Coastal weather makes this worse. The combination of salt air, persistent moisture, and wind-driven rain strips the binder faster than it would in a sheltered inland location. Homeowners throughout Lincoln County — from Waldport up to Lincoln City — deal with this issue more often than they might expect. A quality sealcoat bonds to the remaining surface and locks the aggregate back in place before the deterioration gets out of hand.

5. It Has Been 3 to 5 Years Since the Last Sealcoat

Even if your driveway looks fine at a glance, time alone is reason enough to reseal. Industry standards recommend sealcoating every three to five years under normal conditions. On the Oregon Coast, we lean toward the shorter end of that range because of the accelerated wear from our climate.

Think of sealcoating like painting the exterior of your house. You would not wait until the wood is rotting to repaint — you stay on a maintenance cycle to prevent damage from getting a foothold. The same logic applies to your driveway. A fresh sealcoat every three years is far cheaper than patching cracks, repairing base damage, or replacing the entire surface.

If you are not sure when your driveway was last sealed — or if it has never been sealed at all — that is a strong sign it is time.

Why Coastal Climate Demands Extra Attention

We have mentioned the coastal factor throughout this article because it genuinely matters. Living in Lincoln County means your driveway faces a triple threat that inland properties simply do not deal with:

  • Salt air corrodes the asphalt binder and accelerates oxidation, especially within a few miles of the shoreline in towns like Depoe Bay and Newport.
  • Heavy rainfall keeps surfaces wet for months at a time, promoting water infiltration, erosion, and biological growth like moss and algae.
  • UV exposure breaks down the surface oils even on overcast days, and our long summer daylight hours compound the effect during dry season.

These three factors working together mean that an unsealed driveway on the Oregon Coast can deteriorate two to three times faster than one in Portland or the Willamette Valley. Regular driveway maintenance in Lincoln County is not optional — it is what separates a surface that lasts 20 years from one that needs replacement in under 10.

If you are planning other exterior work, it is worth considering the bigger picture. Projects like hardscaping and patio improvements or even new home construction all benefit from the same attention to coastal-rated materials and proper maintenance schedules.
