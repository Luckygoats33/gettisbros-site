Hardscape Ideas That Thrive on the Oregon Coast

Materials and designs built to handle rain, salt air, and coastal winds.

July 15, 2025 Gettis Bros Team 5 min read

Living on the Oregon Coast means dealing with weather that most hardscape materials were never designed for. Between the constant rain, salt-laden wind, and soil that stays wet for months at a time, the wrong patio or retaining wall can start crumbling within a few years. The right one? It gets better with age.

We have been building hardscapes across Lincoln County since the 1960s—in Newport, Lincoln City, Depoe Bay, Toledo, and everywhere in between. Here is what we have learned about choosing materials and designs that actually hold up out here.

The Best Materials for Wet, Salty Climates

Not every hardscape material belongs on the coast. Some look great in the showroom but fall apart after two winters of rain and salt exposure. These three consistently perform well in our climate:

  • Concrete pavers. Interlocking concrete pavers are one of the most durable options for coastal patios and walkways. They handle freeze-thaw cycles well, resist cracking under ground movement, and individual pavers can be replaced if one gets damaged. For properties in Newport and Depoe Bay that catch direct ocean spray, we recommend pavers with a higher PSI rating—at least 8,000—for maximum salt resistance.
  • Natural stone. Basalt, granite, and bluestone all handle coastal moisture without spalling or flaking. Basalt is a particularly good fit here because it is locally sourced across Oregon, which keeps costs down and gives your hardscape a natural connection to the landscape. Sandstone and limestone, on the other hand, are too porous for our climate and will degrade faster.
  • Stamped concrete. A well-poured, properly sealed stamped concrete patio gives you the look of natural stone or brick at a lower price point. The key on the coast is the sealer—you need to reseal every two to three years to keep moisture from working its way in and causing surface pitting. Done right, stamped concrete patios in Lincoln City and Toledo can last 25 years or more.

If you are weighing options for your property, our hardscaping and concrete services page covers the full range of what we build and install.

Design Ideas That Work on the Coast

Good materials are only half the equation. The design itself needs to account for how coastal homeowners actually use their outdoor spaces—and how the weather treats those spaces year-round.

Patios with purpose. A covered or partially covered patio extends your usable outdoor season by months. We build a lot of patios in the 300 to 500 square-foot range that include a roof overhang or pergola on one side, so homeowners can sit outside even when it is drizzling. Concrete pavers or stamped concrete work well here because they drain quickly and resist moss better than poured concrete alone.

Walkways that handle the rain. Permeable paver walkways are a smart choice for coastal properties because they let water pass through instead of pooling on the surface. This is especially important for hillside homes in Depoe Bay and along the Newport bluffs where runoff can erode the surrounding landscape. A well-designed walkway connects your driveway, patio, and garden areas while keeping mud off your shoes all winter long.

Fire pits for year-round use. There is nothing better than a fire pit on a cool coastal evening, and a built-in hardscape fire pit adds both function and value. We typically build them with concrete block or natural stone and set them into a paver patio for a clean, integrated look. Wind screens or a partial enclosure wall on the windward side make all the difference when the breeze picks up off the ocean.

Retaining walls that do real work. On the coast, retaining walls are not just decorative—they are structural. Hillside properties in Toledo and throughout Lincoln County need walls that manage soil movement, redirect water, and prevent erosion. We use interlocking concrete block or stacked natural stone with proper drainage behind the wall, including gravel backfill and perforated drain pipe. A retaining wall done right protects your foundation and creates usable flat space on a sloped lot. Learn more about how we handle the groundwork at our excavation and site prep page.

What to Avoid Near the Ocean

Some materials that work fine inland will fail quickly on the coast. Here is what we steer clients away from:

  • Limestone and sandstone. Both are porous and absorb salt water, which causes them to flake, pit, and crumble within a few seasons. We have torn out several limestone patios that were only four or five years old.
  • Untreated wood borders and edging. Even pressure-treated lumber breaks down fast in our climate. If you want wood accents, use a composite material or stick to stone and concrete for anything that touches the ground.
  • Cheap concrete without proper reinforcement. Thin poured concrete with no rebar or fiber mesh will crack quickly on the coast, especially on clay-heavy soils that shift with moisture. This is one area where cutting corners costs more in the long run.
  • Metal edging without corrosion protection. Standard steel edging rusts out in a single winter near the ocean. If you use metal, go with galvanized or powder-coated aluminum.

Drainage: The Most Important Detail

Lincoln County averages over 70 inches of rain a year. Every hardscape project we take on starts with a drainage plan, not as an afterthought but as the foundation of the design.

Proper grading is the first step. Every patio and walkway needs a minimum slope of one-quarter inch per foot away from your home's foundation. For larger patios, we install channel drains or French drains along the edges to move water away quickly. Permeable pavers are another option—they let rain pass through the surface and into a gravel base beneath, which reduces puddles and ice in winter.

For retaining walls, drainage behind the wall is non-negotiable. Without it, water pressure builds up and pushes the wall forward over time. We install gravel backfill and a perforated drain pipe at the base of every wall we build. If your property has existing drainage problems, our team can evaluate the site before any hardscape work begins—just reach out for a free consultation.

How Hardscaping Adds Value to Coastal Homes

Beyond the day-to-day benefits of a usable patio or a walkway that keeps you out of the mud, hardscaping is one of the highest-return outdoor investments you can make. Well-built outdoor living spaces typically return 50 to 75 percent of their cost at resale, and on the coast, that number can go higher because buyers know that quality outdoor construction is hard to find and expensive to redo.

A paver patio, a fire pit area, and a solid retaining wall tell a buyer that the previous owner invested in the property and did it right. Compare that to a crumbling concrete slab or a rotting wood deck—those signal deferred maintenance and cost the seller at negotiation time.

Whether you are building for yourself or building equity, hardscaping that is designed for the coast pays for itself in comfort and long-term value. If you are planning a new construction project, it is worth including hardscape in the initial build rather than adding it later.
