Sheds & Outdoor Structures

The Complete Guide to Custom Sheds on the Oregon Coast

Permits, materials, and costs — everything you need before building.

By Gettis Bros Team

If you’re a homeowner on the Oregon Coast, a custom shed is one of the most practical investments you can make. Whether you need extra storage, a dedicated workshop, or a place to keep your gear out of the rain, a well-built shed pays for itself in convenience alone. But building on the coast isn’t like building inland. The salt air, wind, and relentless moisture here in Lincoln County demand materials and methods that pre-fab kits simply don’t deliver.

This guide covers everything you need to know before breaking ground—from permits and foundations to materials and costs—so you can make confident decisions and avoid the mistakes we see homeowners make every year.

Permits & Regulations in Lincoln County

Before you pour a foundation or swing a hammer, you need to understand Lincoln County’s permitting requirements. The rules vary depending on the size of your structure and where your property sits.

  • Under 200 square feet: Most sheds under 200 sq ft do not require a building permit in Lincoln County, but you still need to meet setback requirements. Don’t assume “no permit” means “no rules.”
  • 200 square feet and over: Any shed at or above 200 sq ft requires a building permit. You’ll need a site plan, and the structure must meet Oregon Residential Specialty Code standards.
  • Setback requirements: In most residential zones, sheds must sit at least 5 feet from side and rear property lines. Properties in Newport, Lincoln City, Toledo, or Depoe Bay may have additional overlay zone rules—especially near the shoreline or in flood zones.
  • Coastal overlay zones: If your lot falls within a coastal hazard zone or a geologic setback area, you may face additional review. Properties near the ocean or on bluffs often require a geotechnical report before any construction.

We handle the permit process for every shed we build. If you’re not sure where your property stands, get in touch and we’ll walk you through it before any work begins.

Size Options: From Storage to Full Workshop

Custom sheds aren’t one-size-fits-all, and that’s the whole point. Here are the most common sizes we build for homeowners across Lincoln County:

  • Small storage (8×10 to 10×12): Ideal for lawn equipment, seasonal gear, and basic yard tools. Fits most side yards without a permit.
  • Mid-size utility (10×16 to 12×16): Room for a workbench, power tools, and organized storage. Popular with homeowners in Newport and Lincoln City who need more than a closet but less than a garage.
  • Large workshop (12×20 to 16×24): A true workspace. Electrical, lighting, shelving, and enough floor area to run a table saw or work on projects year-round. Many homeowners in Toledo and Depoe Bay use these as hobby shops, art studios, or home offices.

Every shed we build is custom-framed to your lot and your needs. If you want a loft, side lean-to, or extra-wide door for ATV storage, we design it in from the start—not as an afterthought.

Materials That Survive Coastal Weather

Material selection is where coastal sheds live or die. The Oregon Coast throws everything at an outdoor structure: salt-laden wind, driving rain, moss, and humidity that doesn’t quit from October through June. Here’s what we use and why:

  • Pressure-treated lumber: All framing and structural members are treated for ground contact and moisture resistance. Standard SPF framing won’t last five years out here.
  • Metal roofing: Standing-seam or corrugated metal is our go-to for shed roofs. It sheds rain instantly, resists moss better than asphalt shingles, and handles coastal wind loads without lifting. We’ve seen composition roofs on pre-fab sheds peel off in their first winter storm.
  • Moisture-resistant siding: LP SmartSide, fiber cement, or pre-primed engineered wood. We avoid raw T1-11 and untreated plywood—they absorb moisture and rot from the inside out within a few seasons.
  • Stainless or hot-dipped galvanized hardware: Every fastener, hinge, and bracket is rated for corrosive environments. Standard zinc-plated hardware rusts and fails fast in salt air.

The upfront cost of quality materials is always less than the cost of replacing a shed that falls apart in three years. We’ve seen it dozens of times, and it’s the main reason homeowners call us after their pre-fab kit shed fails.

Workshop vs. Storage: What Are You Really Building?

This is the question we ask every customer before we start designing. A storage shed and a workshop have very different requirements:

If it’s primarily storage, you can keep costs down with a simpler layout—double doors for easy access, basic shelving, and good ventilation to prevent mildew. A gravel pad foundation works well, and you can skip electrical in most cases.

If it’s a working shop, you’ll want a concrete slab for a solid, level floor. Plan for a 200-amp subpanel or at minimum a dedicated 20-amp circuit for power tools. Insulation makes a huge difference if you plan to work through winter—and on the Oregon Coast, that’s half the year. We also recommend at least one window for natural light and cross-ventilation.

Many homeowners start with “I just need some storage” and realize six months later they want a workspace. It’s always cheaper to build it right the first time than to retrofit later, so think about where you’ll be in two or three years.

Foundation Options

Your foundation choice depends on shed size, intended use, and your lot’s drainage. Here are the three most common options we install:

  • Gravel pad: The most affordable option. A compacted gravel base provides drainage and keeps the shed off wet ground. Works well for small to mid-size storage sheds. We grade the pad for positive drainage so water moves away from the structure, not under it.
  • Concrete slab: The best choice for workshops and any shed over 200 sq ft. A 4-inch slab with wire mesh gives you a solid, level, permanent floor. Worth the investment if you’re running power tools or storing anything heavy.
  • Pier blocks: Concrete pier blocks elevate the shed frame off the ground, allowing air circulation underneath. Good for sloped lots and sites with drainage issues. We use these frequently on hillside properties in Depoe Bay and Newport where grading for a slab would be impractical.

Getting the foundation right is the most important part of the build. A shed with a bad foundation settles, doors stop closing, and water gets in. We see this constantly with pre-fab kits that come with no real foundation plan.

Cost Considerations & Value

Custom shed costs vary widely based on size, materials, and features. Here’s a general range for what we build in Lincoln County:

  • Basic storage shed (8×10 to 10×12): $4,000–$7,000
  • Mid-size utility shed (10×16 to 12×16): $7,000–$14,000
  • Large workshop with electrical (12×20+): $14,000–$25,000+

These numbers include materials, labor, foundation prep, and a structure built to last decades in coastal conditions. A custom shed built right is a 25- to 30-year investment. Compare that to a $3,000 pre-fab kit that needs replacing in 5 to 7 years, and the math speaks for itself.

A well-built shed also adds real value to your property. Buyers on the coast know the difference between a solid outbuilding and a sagging box in the backyard.

Why Pre-Fab & Kit Sheds Fail on the Coast

We’re not here to trash the big-box shed kits, but we do see the results year after year. Here’s why they don’t hold up on the Oregon Coast:

  • Untreated lumber: Most kits use standard framing lumber with no pressure treatment. In our climate, that means rot within three to five years—sometimes sooner.
  • Lightweight roofing: Thin asphalt or rolled roofing can’t handle sustained coastal winds. We’ve seen entire roofs peel off kit sheds after a single November storm.
  • No foundation plan: Kits assume you’ll figure out the foundation. Most people set them on bare ground or a few landscape timbers. Within a year, the shed settles unevenly, doors jam, and moisture wicks up from below.
  • Generic hardware: Zinc-plated hinges and fasteners corrode fast in salt air. Doors sag, latches freeze, and panels loosen.
  • No local code compliance: Kit sheds are designed for average conditions. They don’t account for Lincoln County wind loads, coastal building code requirements, or the specific setback and zoning rules in Newport, Lincoln City, or unincorporated county areas.

If you’ve already dealt with a failing pre-fab shed, you know exactly what we’re talking about. We replace more kit sheds than we’d like to count.
